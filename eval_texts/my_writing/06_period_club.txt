My first teen talk in fifth grade was separate for girls and boys to learn about each other. After the boys came out of their classroom, each one was laughing and the reason was obvious: they were laughing about periods. I was stunned by their reaction but I knew it was taboo to speak about. As I grew into a young woman, the “harmless” and “inoffensive” jokes continued which highlighted people’s lack of knowledge and respect towards menstruation. I realized I didn’t know much either but knew its infamy needed to change.

During the club fair of my freshman year, I went to read about what PERIOD club was but found it was still a stigmatized topic. However, it was about normalizing periods by openly educating others; I instantly joined. Every Wednesday for two years, I educated myself about my menstrual cycle and its different aspects. I became more knowledgeable about my own body and empathize with others who have periods.

I realized having easy access to products was a privilege. After becoming president of PERIOD club, I shifted my focus from educational presentations to hosting packing parties to fight period poverty. Period poverty affects people globally because feminine products are too expensive and are considered luxury items. These packing parties allowed members to come in during lunchtime and pack products, cards, and chocolate in paper bags to send to homeless shelters. I also had a donation week where people could come and donate products for packing parties and the club received over 100 products.

Therefore, I realized we have a similar problem at my school. Oftentimes, my friends and I found ourselves stranded in bathrooms with no feminine products to use. After being part of the club, I worked on having jars of different feminine products in every classroom to destigmatize the topic in school so everyone has equal access to them.
This club has allowed me to appreciate the resources I have as a female and connected me with others who wish to help this movement. My goal is a future with menstrual equity.
