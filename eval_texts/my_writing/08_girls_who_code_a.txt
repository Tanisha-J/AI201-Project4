I first began my journey in Girls Who Code in which I was exposed to several different coding languages with girls all about my age and learned how to create basic websites and apps. My first project revolved around one of the biggest issues I had as an eleven-year-old: “what should I wear?” Through this project, I created an app “Wardrobe Wizard” which would help you choose outfits by randomly selecting pieces of clothing from your closet.

However, I realized that no project was an “one-and-done” when I was required to get feedback from my peers. At first, I underestimated the power of iterations, thinking everyone had something negative to say about my app. Over time, I realized how crucial user feedback was for improving the interface and achieving optimal efficiency.

To explore the hardware side of computer science, I shifted towards 3D printing a voice-controlled amputee arm for a science fair project. I was excited to move beyond simulations and online results to create something tangible as the next step in advancing my software experience. I used all the skills I had learned while exploring different aspects of computer science, including 3D printing, design, coding, and circuitry. Although this was a lengthy learning curve because I had to adapt to new software and build components instead of coding them, I enjoyed learning this skill and eventually teaching it to the rest of my team.
