Sadness understands that Bing Bong feels grief over his loss and that he needs to be consoled, understood, and validated instead of having Joy tip toe around his emotions and not cry. Both scenes reduce the need for optimism. Wyatt Moss-Wellington from University of Nottingham Ningbo China explains that “Growing up sometimes means experiencing loss and feeling sorrow about such loss”. As these are normal experiences, Joy is not needed to push around the sorrow and make each memory happy but rather needs Sadness as comfort and validation. Moss-Wellington also states that “The experience of positive emotions begins to drop precipitously in frequency and intensity around age 11” which aligns with the story as Riley and her parents see a decline in Riley’s happy memories but understand that she cannot possibly be happy all the time, especially compared to when she was a child. It also emphasizes that, similar to Sadness, other emotions need to be taken into consideration instead of hyperfocusing on one. Through these scenes, children understand that we need to acknowledge all of our emotions, however, acknowledgement isn’t always enough.
