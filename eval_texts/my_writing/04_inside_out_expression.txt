Another key point of the film is that Riley needs to voice her thoughts and feelings instead of keeping them in. Although acceptance is crucial to understand oneself, we need people around us to understand us too so they can help and support us. In fear of misunderstanding or abandonment, oftentimes children do not speak up when they are feeling heavy emotions. When speaking about misunderstanding, it doesn’t always mean being misunderstood but it also means misunderstanding themselves because the feelings are so intense within them. A good practice would be having a safe place for them to express themselves. An example of this within Inside Out is after Riley attempts to run away from home in San Francisco back to Minnesota.

This exemplifies the idea of trust and expression of what she was truly feeling instead of masking it because she believed her parents should know the truth. In turn, she was given comfort and validation outwardly. As mentioned above, we all need validation from each other as much as we need validation internally when accepting emotions. Lori Plutchik from Psychology Today identifies the need for the “evolving capacity of young children to understand, articulate, and regulate their emotions” and “the capacity to understand and regulate one's emotions and empathize with others”, both essential to emotional competence and having a healthy balance between all feelings. Referring back to the scene, Riley first understands that the way she’s been acting irrationally is because of her big emotions tied to moving across the country. Then she is able to articulate by coming clean to her parents and stating that she does miss Minnesota. Lastly, she is able to regulate her emotions by allowing her parents to hold and comfort her. This is a prime example of Plutchik’s concept of how to express emotions in a healthy way, further emphasizing that expression is needed to be accepted by others.
