One may argue that the creators oversimplified Riley’s emotions by only portraying 5 and doesn’t show the brain as something complex. According to Movie Review, an unbiased website for movie reviews, explains that “While Joy, Sadness, Anger, Fear, and Disgust are considered core emotions, other emotions, such as surprise, shame, and guilt, could certainly have been included”. Although true, Director Pete Docter had consulted many neuroscientists and psychologists to keep this selected few in the movie because this is a movie directed towards children. Furthermore, four new emotions foreshadowed in the movie that appeared in the sequel, conveying that as Riley grows older and matures, emotions become more complex. The brain was not complicated and as a study from Cornell University’s neurons and cognition department states, “results from neuroscience approaches suggest a multi-processes mechanism underpinning emotional experience with a large overlap across different emotions”, explaining that feelings are not separated and are caused by an overlap of different emotions rather than one singular emotion taking control. In contrast, this happens to be the one of the main points stated within the movie. As seen in the beginning of the movie, Joy often takes control of the brain activity and when is absent, the other emotions are unaware of what to do, however, at the end of the movie all emotions are working, proving that the overlap is necessary and how the brain is complex.

Inside Out brings a child-friendly perspective to increase EQ by overall emotional acceptance and balanced expression. The film gives examples of when Joy attempts to overpower the other emotions, specifically Sadness, but realizes that Sadness, as all the other emotions, have their place and should be expressed rather than suppressed. Additionally, Riley learns that expressing her emotions leads to healthy conflict resolution as well as reassurance that her big feelings are being understood. Although critics may claim that this film oversimplifies feelings and emotions, it does not take away from its foundation to teach children mental well being. By portraying these five key emotions going through grief and loss as well as growing up and new experiences, Inside Out conveys that we cannot avoid difficult feelings but instead to feel and understand them for an overall happy life.
