# Milestone 1 — Trace a submission

> Drafted with Claude (AI), based on the repo's docstrings and config. Read, checked, and edited by me.

## 1. The path a piece of text takes

1. **`POST /submit` in `app.py`** receives JSON with `text` and `creator_id`.
   Adds: the raw text and who claims to have written it.
2. **Input check.** If `text` is missing, not a string, empty, or too long, stop here and return a 400 with an error message (and log the rejection).
   Adds: a guarantee that everything after this point gets real text.
3. **Content ID.** The route generates a random `content_id` (uuid).
   Adds: a handle the writer can use later to appeal or check status.
4. **Signal one — `detector.py::model_signal`.** gpt2 measures how predictable the text is (perplexity).
   Adds: `model_score`, 0–1, higher = more AI-like.
5. **Signal two — `stylometry.py::style_signal`.** Measures the shape of the text: sentence-length spread, unique-word ratio, punctuation density.
   Adds: `style_score`, 0–1, higher = more AI-like.
6. **Combine — `scoring.py::combine_signals`.** Merges the two scores using the weights in `config.py`.
   Adds: one `confidence` number, 0–1.
7. **Label — `scoring.py::score_to_label`.** Turns the number into a guess (`ai` / `human` / `unsure`) and a plain-English sentence, using the thresholds in `config.py`.
   Adds: what the reader actually sees.
8. **Log, then respond.** `audit.py::log_decision` writes all of it (both signal scores, the combined score, the label, status `decided`) to `logs/audit.jsonl`, then the route returns the JSON.
   Adds: a permanent record. Logging happens *before* responding so that no decision exists without a trace.

## 2. The two signals and their blind spots

### Signal one — model signal (`detector.py`)
- **Measures:** how easy the text is for a language model (gpt2) to predict, word by word.
- **Why it might differ:** AI writes by picking likely next words, so its text is easy for a model to predict. People make odder, more specific choices.
- **What it can't see:** who actually wrote it. It rewards common words in plain sentences, and many real people write that way: second-language writers, younger writers, anyone writing simply on purpose. They score as machine-like. The reverse also happens: AI text dressed up with rare, fancy words can score as human.

### Signal two — style signal (`stylometry.py`)
- **Measures:** the shape of the text, not the meaning. Do sentences vary in length? How much vocabulary repeats? How much punctuation is there?
- **Why it might differ:** AI prose tends to be even, with similar mid-length sentences and a narrower vocabulary. Human writing is lumpier: a three-word sentence next to a forty-word one.
- **What it can't see:** what the text says. Cheap surface edits move it without changing authorship: breaking up sentences, adding a few typos or dashes, pasting in a quote. Someone can make AI text look human in 30 seconds. And poetry has its own shape (short lines, repetition on purpose), which can look "even" for reasons that have nothing to do with AI.

## 3. Following a false positive

- **Who:** Mei, a student writing in English as her second language. She wrote a short poem herself, using simple words she's confident about, in short, regular lines.
- **Signal one:** probably **high (~0.75)**. Simple, common words are exactly what gpt2 predicts easily.
- **Signal two:** probably **medium-high (~0.6)**. Her lines are similar lengths, and she repeats words she's sure of, so her vocabulary looks narrow.
- **Combined (0.5 / 0.5):** about **0.68**, just over the 0.65 AI threshold.
- **Label she sees:** the high-confidence AI label, something like "This was probably generated by AI."
- **How she feels:** accused of cheating on something she worked hard on, by a system she can't argue with, for writing the way she can.
- **What she can do:** `POST /appeal` with her `content_id` and her reasoning. Status moves to `under_review`, and she can check it with `GET /content/<id>`. Nothing gets re-scored automatically; a person has to look.
- **What this means for thresholds:** she lands just over the line, where both signals agree for the wrong reason. A 0.65 cutoff is too easy to reach for someone accused of cheating. The "AI" label should need a higher score (closer to 0.75–0.8), and the unsure band should be wide, so borderline people like Mei get "unsure" instead of an accusation. The cost: more AI text gets "unsure" instead of "AI". On a writing site, that's the right trade.

## 4. Endpoint contracts

### `POST /submit`
**In (JSON):**
| Field | Type | Required | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text` | string, 1–10,000 chars after trimming | yes | `"The rain kept its own time..."` |
| `creator_id` | non-empty string | yes | `"writer_42"` |

**Out — 200:**
| Field | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `content_id` | string (uuid) | `"a3f9c1e2-..."` |
| `guess` | `"ai"` / `"human"` / `"unsure"` | `"unsure"` |
| `confidence` | float 0–1, higher = more AI-like | `0.58` |
| `label` | string, full sentence for the reader | `"We can't tell..."` |
| `model_score` | float 0–1 | `0.71` |
| `style_score` | float 0–1 | `0.45` |
| `status` | `"decided"` | `"decided"` |

**Out — 400:** `{"error": "invalid_input", "message": "<what was wrong>"}` when `text` or `creator_id` is missing, empty, the wrong type, or `text` is over the length limit. Never a 500.

### `POST /appeal`
**In (JSON):**
| Field | Type | Required | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `content_id` | string from a `/submit` response | yes | `"a3f9c1e2-..."` |
| `reasoning` | non-empty string | yes | `"I wrote this myself; here are my drafts."` |

**Out — 200:** `{"content_id": "...", "status": "under_review", "message": "Your appeal was received. A person will review it."}`
**Out — 400:** `{"error": "invalid_input", ...}` if either field is missing or empty.
**Out — 404:** `{"error": "not_found", ...}` if the `content_id` isn't in the log.

### `GET /content/<id>` (stretch, already built)
**Out — 200:** `content_id`, `creator_id`, `status` (`decided` or `under_review`), `submitted_at`, `updated_at`, `decision` {`guess`, `confidence`, `label`, `model_score`, `style_score`}, `appeals` [{`timestamp`, `reasoning`}].
**Out — 404:** `{"error": "not_found", ...}`
